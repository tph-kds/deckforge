# Presentation Archetypes

Select an archetype before choosing a template. The archetype represents the communication situation; the template represents a reusable narrative recipe.

Read `assets/presentation-archetype-manifest.json`.

## Selection inputs

- audience role and familiarity;
- decision or learning outcome;
- duration;
- evidence type;
- presenter-led versus self-guided delivery;
- domain constraints;
- expected level of interaction;
- whether the content is persuasive, explanatory, instructional, or evaluative.

## Supported archetypes

DeckForge includes archetypes for:

- startup and investor pitches;
- executive and board decisions;
- product launch and sales narratives;
- technical architecture and engineering reviews;
- seminar and conference talks;
- academic research and thesis defense;
- education lessons and training workshops;
- data storytelling and business reports;
- portfolio and case-study showcases;
- public policy and impact communication;
- product demos;
- internal all-hands and change programs.

## Adaptation rules

Do not force every topic into problem → solution → benefits → conclusion.

Examples:

- A lesson needs learning objectives, concept scaffolding, practice, checks, and recap.
- A seminar needs thesis, context, argument/evidence, implications, and discussion.
- A technical review needs constraints, system context, alternatives, chosen design, risks, rollout, and observability.
- A pitch needs credible problem evidence, differentiated solution, market/traction proof, business model, ask, and risks.
- An executive update needs decision framing, variance, causes, options, recommendation, owner, and next milestone.

## Quality check

The final slide map must match the audience's real information need. Remove template slides that do not support the objective. Add appendix or references when evidence density is high.
