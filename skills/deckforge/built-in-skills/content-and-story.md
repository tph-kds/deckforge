# Content and Story

Build the argument before selecting a visual treatment. A deck is a sequence of decisions and evidence, not a set of decorated pages.

## Discovery contract

Capture the audience, objective, decision or action requested, presentation duration, delivery context, available evidence, brand constraints, language, and tone. Separate known facts from assumptions and unsupported claims. When the source material is incomplete, use visible placeholders rather than inventing facts.

## Narrative workflow

1. Write a one-sentence thesis that could serve as the final takeaway.
2. Choose an arc appropriate to the task: situation–complication–resolution, problem–evidence–proposal, context–choice–plan, question–method–result, or demonstration–proof–next step.
3. Create a slide map before writing slide bodies. Each entry must include a narrative role, a claim-led title, evidence, visual form, intended build behavior, speaker note, and source requirement.
4. Ensure every slide answers one audience question and advances the thesis. Remove duplicate roles, agenda filler, generic section titles, and slides that exist only because a template contained them.
5. Put presenter explanation, edge cases, and long methodology in speaker notes or an appendix. Keep the visible slide focused on what must be seen together.
6. End with a specific decision, commitment, recommendation, or next action rather than a generic thank-you slide.

## Writing rules

Use claim-led titles such as “Activation improved after guided setup,” not labels such as “Results.” Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Avoid paragraph walls, unexplained acronyms, vague superlatives, and ornamental statistics. Keep terminology stable across slides.

## Evidence and trust

Every external claim, metric, comparison, quotation, or image attribution needs a traceable source ID. State uncertainty, time range, denominator, units, and methodology where they affect interpretation. Never fabricate testimonials, logos, market sizes, experiment results, or citations.

## Completion check

Read only the titles in sequence. They should form a coherent argument. Then verify that each visual is the simplest evidence for its claim and that the final action follows logically from the preceding slides.
